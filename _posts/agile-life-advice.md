Here are my lessons from Agile, phrased as life advice.

The software industry chased "Agile" for a decades without really understanding what it was about.
I disagree with most of what was attributed to that moniker.
And I am sad, because the Agile that I saw in 2003 held so much promise but instead "Agile" was used as a bludgeon to worsen the lives of software people everywhere.
As a result, it is no longer worthwhile to encourage people and organizations to adopt "Agile", but the values I 

- self-care and self-determination are paramount

- most of my ideas are bad ideas, but I can't tell which is which until they get to production

If someone has an idea that I think is a bad idea, I want to try the idea anyway (unless it is obviously harmful/dangerous). Maybe I’m wrong and it will be great and I’ll learn something. Maybe I’m right and that will reveal itself without me being an ass about it.

I am pretty much always wrong about how long something will take, usually by a lot and on the "longer than expected" direction

It is better to work on one thing to completion than two things in parallel, even though this hurts.

When it comes to a backlog, I usually only want to see items that I expect to have real "customer" value

the purpose of the ordered backlog is:
- the first item tells us what to focus on now while ignoring everything else
- the second item tells us when the first item is GEFN and we should stop ignoring everything else
- the third item is just there as a point of reference, to ground us
- most of the rest of the list will probably never happen (this is ok). time/effort/attention you put on these is (probably) wasted
- when a new idea comes up, ask if it should displace the first, second, or third item, or just throw it anywhere on the list

- a backlog is effectively infinite. If the thing we are working on is worthwhile and we care, we will constantly come up with new ideas faster than we complete them. so don't stress about TODO lists never shrinking

before working on a hard thing, ask:
- is there a smaller piece of this I can do first? (there always is)
- is there something I can change that will make this thing less hard (there always is)

When something goes awry, in addition to fixing it, ask "what small change would make it easier to avoid, diagnose, or address things like this in the future?"
