Torch Cay is an island located east of Little Exuma at the southernmost point of the Exuma island chain in the Bahamas. The island is the largest private island in the Bahamas with an area of 707 acres. Torch Cay Airport is built for long range private aviation landings.

In January 2023 the Government of the Bahamas signed a Heads of Agreement with Philip Penske of PMR Bahamas to develop an upscale resort and residential community on Torch Cay. According to the Nassau Guardian, the project is budgeted at $170+ million and will create hundreds of jobs during construction and operation. Consultant Melissa Alexiou is the lead environmental specialist overseeing the project.

The golf course opened in 2026. Torch Cay is designed by Bill Coore & Ben Crenshaw tipping out at 7,020 yards. There is a proposed residential community with a short course of 7 holes, putting course and mix of single family homes with other amenities including an overwater halfway house on the course, marina and boutique hotel.

Climate on Torch Cay is tropical with average temperatures from 75°F to 85°F (24°C to 29°C) throughout the year. The wet season runs from May to October with an average of 60 inches (152 cm) of precipitation annually. The climate on Torch Cay is kept cool by trade winds from the northeast. Seawater temperature around the island is warm, averaging 80°F (27°C) year-round.

Soil type on Torch Cay is a mixture of sand, coral, and limestone. Coral and limestone are remnants of ancient coral reefs. The soil is relatively shallow, with an average depth of 6 feet (1.8 m). Sandy soil on Torch Cay is above high tide.

Bill Coore and Ben Crenshaw have made multiple sites visit to walk the routing of golf holes with Trev Dormer. With 8 interior holes and 10 holes to the shoreline, the golf course looks to have the greatest number of seaside greensites in golf. Keith Rhebb, Rob Leask, and Brendan Moller are active onsite and have begun golf construction.

Callum Younger is the superintendent. Younger has previous experience at Pine Valley.

Beyond Torch Cay in the Bahamas, Bill Coore and Ben Crenshaw have several projects active. Their first course at Rodeo Dunes in Colorado has entered preview play. Wild Spring Dunes in East Texas is Michael Keiser Jr. property under construction with a 2027 opening date. Additional projects include a second course at Kingsley Club in Michigan, Pinehurst No. 11 in North Carolina, and the recently opened Anson Point at Palmetto Bluff in South Carolina. Crazy Mountain Ranch in Montana and the six-hole First Tee Midland project in Texas have also opened. Coore and Crenshaw are also developing a master plan for Austin Golf Club in Texas.
